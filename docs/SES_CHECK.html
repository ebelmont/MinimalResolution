Deducing the E2 page of S/α₁ from the sphere's#

There is no published Adams–Novikov E₂ chart for S/α₁ at p=3 to check mr_BP_comod's output against. There doesn't need to be: the cofiber sequence determines it from the sphere's page, which is known (and which this repo computes). This page derives the answer, explains where the non-trivial comodule structure goes, and describes ses_check.py, which carries the comparison out automatically.

1. The short exact sequence#

α₁ ∈ π₃(S)₍₃₎ is the first alpha-family element, 2p-3 = 3 at p=3, and S/α₁ = cofib(S³ --α₁--> S⁰) = S⁰ ∪_{α₁} e⁴. Since BP_* is concentrated in even degrees, BP₃ = 0, so (α₁)_* = 0 on BP-homology and the cofiber long exact sequence collapses to

0 --> BP_*  -->  BP_*(S/α₁)  -->  Σ⁴ BP_*  --> 0.            (†)

The shift is 4, not 3. The subquotient is the reduced BP-homology of the top cell, which sits in dimension 3 + 1 = 4 — S³ is the source of the attaching map, not a cell of the complex. This is worth pinning down because the parity argument depends on it: Σ⁴BP_* is again concentrated in even degrees, consistent with BP_*(S/α₁) being free on generators in degrees 0 and 4 (comodules.cpp build_S_alpha1 sets degree = {0, 4}). A Σ³ would have put the top cell in odd degrees and broken the parity that makes (†) exact in the first place.

2. Two different splittings, and why only one of them is true#

This is the crux of the question this page exists to answer.

(†) is split over BP_*: both BP_* and Σ⁴BP_* are free, so choosing a BP_*-basis x₀, x₄ splits it as a sequence of BP_*-modules.

(†) is not split over BP_*BP: a comodule splitting would make BP_*(S/α₁) ≅ BP_* ⊕ Σ⁴BP_* with the trivial coaction on both summands, i.e. BP-homology could not tell S/α₁ from S⁰ ∨ S⁴. The coaction

ψ(x₀) = 1 ⊗ x₀
ψ(x₄) = 1 ⊗ x₄  +  t₁ ⊗ x₀

is exactly the record of that failure: x₀ spans a sub-comodule, but no BP_*-complement of it is a sub-comodule, because x₄'s coaction has that off-diagonal t₁.

Both facts are used, and they are used for different things:

  • the BP_*-splitting is what makes (†) stay exact after applying the cobar construction — the cobar complex of M is BP_*BP^{⊗•} ⊗_{BP_*} M, so degreewise BP_*-splitness gives a short exact sequence of cochain complexes, hence a long exact sequence on Ext;
  • the failure of the comodule splitting is what makes that long exact sequence non-degenerate — it is precisely the connecting homomorphism.

So the answer to "how does the non-trivial comodule structure get accounted for?" is: entirely through the connecting map δ, and through nothing else. Everything else in the long exact sequence sees only the sphere.

3. The connecting map is multiplication by α₁#

For any short exact sequence of comodules

0 --> A --> M --> B --> 0

which is split over BP_*, the class of the extension lives in Ext¹_{BP_*BP}(B, A), and the connecting homomorphism in the long exact sequence for Ext_{BP_*BP}(BP_*, −) is Yoneda product with that class. Here

Ext¹_{BP_*BP}(Σ⁴BP_*, BP_*) = Ext^{1,4}_{BP_*BP}(BP_*, BP_*) = Z/3,

generated by h₀ = [t₁] = α₁. The extension class of (†) is a generator — that is what "the extension is non-split" means, and non-splitness holds because α₁ ≠ 0. Concretely, with the BP_*-splitting M = BP_*x₀ ⊕ BP_*x₄ fixed, the extension class is represented by the cobar 1-cochain measuring how far the splitting is from being a comodule map,

x₄  ↦  ψ(x₄) − 1⊗x₄  =  t₁ ⊗ x₀,

i.e. by the cocycle [t₁] — literally the off-diagonal entry of the coaction matrix. (Topologically, the same statement is the geometric boundary theorem: the connecting map for the cofiber sequence of f is multiplication by the class detecting f.) Note the answer is forced up to a unit: in internal degree 4 the reduced part of BP_*BP over BP_* is spanned by t₁ alone, so replacing t₁ by 2t₁ just rescales x₄.

The long exact sequence therefore reads, writing E = Ext_{BP_*BP}(BP_*,BP_*) for the sphere's page,

... --> E^{s,t} --> Ext^{s,t}(BP_*(S/α₁)) --> E^{s,t-4} --α₁--> E^{s+1,t} --> ...

and breaks into short exact sequences

0 --> coker(α₁ : E^{s-1,t-4} → E^{s,t})
  --> Ext^{s,t}(BP_*(S/α₁))
  --> ker(α₁ : E^{s,t-4} → E^{s+1,t})  --> 0.                (‡)

4. In chart coordinates#

Charts here are drawn at (stem, filtration) = (t−s, s), so:

bidegree shift
multiplication by α₁ (stem +3, s +1)
top-cell classes (Ext^{s,t}(Σ⁴BP_*) = Ext^{s,t-4}(BP_*)) (stem +4, s +0)

and (‡) becomes the recipe:

**Take the sphere's chart together with its α₁-multiplication lines. Delete every class that is hit by an α₁-line (bottom cell = coker). Then add, four stems to the right and at the same height, a copy of every class that does not support an α₁-line (top cell = ker).**

Two sanity checks fall straight out of this:

  • α₁ itself is hit by an α₁-line from the unit, so it dies — coning off a class kills it.
  • Nothing new appears in stem 4 at filtration 0 for free: ker(α₁ : E^{0,0} → E^{1,4}) is 3·Z₍₃₎ ⊂ Z₍₃₎, not all of Z₍₃₎, because α₁·1 = α₁ ≠ 0. The top-cell class only exists after multiplying by 3 — which is the statement that α₁ has order 3, read off the chart.

5. What the code prints, and why lengths are the right thing to compare#

The tables mr_BP/mr_BP_comod write list an F₃-basis of the algebraic Novikov E∞ page, i.e. of gr Ext_{BP_*BP} — v0-towers included, differential-killed classes excluded. The number of surviving classes in a bidegree is therefore the F₃-length of Ext there:

  • 12 classes in (0,0) = Z₍₃₎ seen through 12 steps of its 3-adic filtration (the count is cut off by the resolution length, not intrinsic);
  • the 2 classes in stem 11, filtration 1 = the Z/9 generated by α_{3/2} (v0^1[1-1], v0^2[1-1]; the a0 table links them).

Length is additive along a short exact sequence, so (‡) predicts

ℓ(S/α₁)(n,s) = [ ℓ_S(n,s) − rk(α₁ into (n,s)) ]
             + [ ℓ_S(n−4,s) − rk(α₁ out of (n−4,s)) ]

with every term readable from a sphere run: the class tables give the lengths, and <prefix>AANSS_h0.txt gives the α₁-multiplication whose ranks appear here.

6. ses_check.py#

./BPtab 35
./mr_BP_comod 35 12 sphere
./mr_BP_comod 35 12 alpha_1
./ses_check.py 35 -c alpha_1

Nothing is recomputed — like anss_chart.py it reads only the text tables the runs already wrote, and it shares that script's parser. Exit status is 1 if any bidegree disagrees, so it works as a regression test.

Every bidegree in range agrees, and every prediction is sharp (no interval — see below). Reading a few lines:

  • stems 3, 13, 23, 29 — α₁, α₁β₁, α₁β₁², α₁β₂ (the targets of the sphere's four h0 lines in range): predicted 0, observed 0. Coning off α₁ kills every class it hits.
  • stem 4: 11, not 12 — the order-3 statement of §4.
  • stems 14, 24, 30: predicted 0 because the sphere classes four stems down — β₁ at (10,2), β₁² at (20,4), β₂ at (26,2) — each support an α₁-line, so they contribute nothing to the kernel and the top cell adds nothing.
  • stem 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27, 31: bottom-cell survivor plus one top-cell class each.

Ranks are bracketed, not guessed#

The multiplication tables are pruned by homological degree (multiplication.cpp:169), so some classes have no line. Rather than assume their products vanish, each rank is reported as an interval lo-hi:

lo = rank of the columns the table does state
hi = min(lo + #unstated columns, #source classes, #target classes)

A bidegree agrees when the observation lies in the predicted interval, and the check is sharp when lo = hi — which it is everywhere above, because the target bidegrees are small or empty.

The control cases#

--attaching none treats the extension as split (δ = 0), which is the right prediction for triv_01 = S ∨ S¹:

./mr_BP_comod 35 12 triv_01
./ses_check.py 35 -c triv_01 --attaching none      # 28 bidegrees, 0 mismatches

The same flag is also the negative control for S/α₁: pretending its extension is split,

./ses_check.py 35 -c alpha_1 --attaching none --top-cell 4

produces 8 mismatches — at stems 3, 4, 13, 14, 23, 24, 29, 30, i.e. at every bidegree where the α₁-multiplication was doing work. So the check has teeth: it is not a computation that would have come out right regardless of the coaction.

The same 8 bidegrees fail from the other direction if the comodule is the wrong one rather than the prediction. Adding a scratch builder with rank = 2, degree = {0, 4} and the identity coaction — S⁰ ∨ S⁴, differing from alpha_1 only by the off-diagonal t₁ — and running the check against the S/α₁ prediction gives

13 bidegree(s) agree, 8 mismatch(es).
  MISMATCH at (stem 3, s=1): predicted 0, observed 1
  MISMATCH at (stem 4, s=0): predicted 11, observed 12
  ... (stems 13, 14, 23, 24, 29, 30)

so the check is sensitive to exactly the entry that carries the topology. (A malformed coaction is a different matter: putting η_R(v₁) in that slot — the exponent-slot mistake comodules.cpp's header warns about — made mr_BP_comod abort in Z3.cpp:112, "tried to invert non-invertible element 3", rather than produce a wrong page. That is one data point, not a guarantee: nothing checks the comodule axioms, and this check cannot see them either.)

7. What this does and does not establish#

It establishes that mr_BP_comod's alpha_1 output has, in every bidegree in range, exactly the F₃-length that the cofiber sequence forces given the sphere's page and its α₁-multiplication — including the killed classes and the shifted top-cell classes, which no plumbing bug would reproduce by accident.

It does not establish:

  • that the coaction satisfies the comodule axioms. Nothing in this pipeline checks coassociativity or counitality (see GENERAL_COMODULES.md); this check would also pass for any comodule with the same Ext.
  • hidden extensions or the module structure. Only lengths are compared. Two different Z/3 ⊕ Z/3 vs Z/9 answers in one bidegree are indistinguishable here — although the a0 tables of the two runs can be compared by hand where that matters.
  • anything above the degree bound. The default cut is the largest internal degree t occurring in the sphere run (32 for halfT=35); above it, classes are missing because of truncation rather than topology (CHARTS.md §6).
  • products that jump algebraic Novikov filtration. The multiplication tables print only the leading term (multiplication.cpp:170-172 outputs multiplied_names.second and drops .first), so an α₁-product hidden in higher filtration would understate a rank and overstate the prediction. No such discrepancy shows up in range.

8. Using this for another complex#

The same script handles any two-cell complex C_f = cofib(f : S^d → S⁰) whose attaching map is detected by a class with a multiplication table: pass --top-cell d+1, and add the multiplication table for the detecting class. Currently only h0 = α₁ is tabulated (BPInit::mult_table, BP_init.cpp:180-187); α₂ would need a mult_table(<class>, <degree>, "alpha2.txt") call and one more branch here, exactly as CHARTS.md §3 describes for the chart.

For a complex with more cells the same principle applies with a longer filtration: filter BP_*(C) by skeleta, and the associated spectral sequence has E₁ a sum of shifted copies of the sphere's Ext with d₁ given by the attaching-map classes. The two-cell case above is that spectral sequence with one differential, written out as a long exact sequence.
